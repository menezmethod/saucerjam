# Trademark and brand policy

> Working policy, not legal advice. SaucerJam is an unregistered mark asserted through
> use; a formal clearance search and filing are pending (see
> [`docs/PUBLIC-LAUNCH-FOUNDATION.md`](docs/PUBLIC-LAUNCH-FOUNDATION.md) §Name strategy).

The code is open source ([`LICENSE`](LICENSE), and see [`LICENSING.md`](LICENSING.md)).
**The brand is not part of that grant.** No code licence — MIT today, AGPL-3.0 if the
project switches — gives anyone rights in the names, logos or domains below.

## 1. What is claimed

- The name **SaucerJam** and the wordmark **SaucerJam / Orbital combat**.
- The SaucerJam logo: the circular saucer-and-dome mark used in the lobby wordmark
  (`src/index.html`) and in the social share image (`src/assets/social/saucerjam-share.png`).
- Official domains and services: **saucerjam.com**, **www.saucerjam.com**,
  **dev.saucerjam.com**, **community.saucerjam.com**, and the official Coolify
  deployments behind them.
- Brand vocabulary: **Join the Jam**, **Jam Night**, **Build a Saucer**,
  **The Archive Opens**.

## 2. Official services

**Operating an official SaucerJam service is reserved to the project.** Neither MIT nor
AGPL-3.0 grants any operational right, endorsement, affiliation or trademark licence.

Concretely:

- The official game is the one served at **saucerjam.com**. It is run by the project
  maintainers.
- A self-hosted instance is a **self-hosted instance**, never "the SaucerJam server",
  "official SaucerJam", or "SaucerJam" unqualified.
- The project does not endorse, support, vouch for or take responsibility for any
  third-party instance, its moderation, its availability, its data handling, or its
  content.
- Being able to read or reuse the source does **not** imply that the project authorises a
  competing official-looking service.
- If and when the project sells commercial hosting or a commercial licence, that channel
  is the project's alone; a third party cannot offer "commercial SaucerJam hosting"
  without a licence from the project.

If the code licence later becomes AGPL-3.0, a modified instance operated for users must
also offer those users the corresponding source (AGPL-3.0 §13). That obligation is a
condition of the software licence; it is not a grant of brand rights.

## 3. Allowed without asking

- Playing, streaming, recording, reviewing, and writing about SaucerJam.
- Linking to <https://saucerjam.com> or <https://community.saucerjam.com>.
- Referring to the game factually by name, including in headlines and titles such as
  "how SaucerJam's drift movement works".
- Running a self-hosted instance, including a modified one, **under a different name and
  without the official logo in its branding or UI**.
- Fan sites, wikis, community tools, tournaments and Discord servers — non-commercial,
  clearly unofficial, no official logo used as their own branding.
- Redistributing an **unmodified** official build, keeping the official branding intact
  and stating the source and version.
- Saying that your project "is built on SaucerJam", "is a fork of SaucerJam", or "uses
  SaucerJam's simulation", as long as it does not read as official.

## 4. Not allowed without written permission

- Naming a modified build, product, service, server, app or store listing **SaucerJam**,
  or a name confusingly similar to it (including "SaucerJam 2", "SaucerJam Online",
  "SaucerJam Classic", "SaucerJamHD").
- Using the logo, wordmark or share image as **your** product's branding, icon, avatar,
  splash screen or store art.
- Claiming, implying or hinting at official status, sponsorship, endorsement, partnership
  or affiliation with the project.
- Registering or using a domain, handle, app-store name or Steam/itch page that imitates
  an official SaucerJam channel.
- Selling merchandise, prints, collectibles or physical goods bearing the mark.
- Using the mark as a company or product name in a way that suggests you are the source of
  the game.

## 5. Forks, and how to do it right

Forking is welcome and explicitly permitted by the code licence. A public fork should:

1. **Rename** the game — product name, page title, PWA manifest name, in-game wordmark.
2. **Replace** the logo and share image, and drop the "Orbital combat" wordmark treatment.
3. **State** clearly that it is an unofficial fork, and of which version, in its README
   and its UI (an "About" note is enough).
4. **Say** "not affiliated with or endorsed by SaucerJam" in the same place.
5. **Keep** the MIT licence notice for the code it inherited.

Community portals (Fider), the official roadmap and the official leaderboards belong to
the canonical project and should not be presented as a fork's official surfaces.

## 6. Contributions and the brand

Contributing code, art, audio or models does **not** grant you rights in the SaucerJam
mark, and does not make you an agent or representative of the project
(see [`CLA.md`](CLA.md) §4). Contributors may factually describe their contribution.

## 7. Marketplace and creator content

Creators keep ownership of what they upload; the project's licence to display and
distribute it is defined in [`docs/MARKETPLACE-TERMS.md`](docs/MARKETPLACE-TERMS.md).
That licence does not extend to the SaucerJam mark. Selling a creator's own creation
outside the marketplace requires the project's permission, because marketplace listings
and the mark travel together.

## 8. Asking

For any use in §4, or anything you are unsure about, open a GitHub Discussion at
<https://github.com/menezmethod/saucerjam/discussions> describing the use, the channel,
and the audience. Approval is at the maintainers' discretion, is specific to the request,
and can be withdrawn.

## 9. Enforcement intent

The project would rather license than litigate. Good-faith fans, forks and community
projects get a conversation. Confusing or commercial use of the mark — a lookalike app, a
domain, a store listing, merch — gets a request to stop, and escalation only if needed.
